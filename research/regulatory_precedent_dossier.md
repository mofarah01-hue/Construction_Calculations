## 0. Executive Summary

The regulatory environment moved in this program's favor on January 6, 2026. On that date FDA issued revised final versions of both the General Wellness guidance and the Clinical Decision Support guidance. The revised General Wellness guidance expressly brings noninvasive wearables that estimate or infer physiologic parameters (blood pressure, blood glucose, blood oxygen saturation, heart rate variability) inside the enforcement discretion lane, provided the product stays wellness framed and avoids disease and diagnostic threshold claims. This is the single most consequential development for both concepts and it is dated inside the current window.

The lane rests on three legs. FDA decides whether the product is a device. The statute (FD&C Act section 520(o), as amended by the 21st Century Cures Act) removes qualifying wellness and decision support software from the device definition entirely. FTC decides whether the claim is true and demands competent and reliable scientific evidence behind every health related representation. Get the output string right and the FDA question resolves. The FTC question does not disappear; it converts into a validation cost that lands at G3 and G4.

| Leg | Regulator or authority | Question it answers | Program consequence |
|-----|------------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | FDA General Wellness policy | Is a low risk wellness product a device FDA will regulate? | Enforcement discretion if wellness framed and low risk |
| 2 | FD&C Act 520(o), 21st Century Cures Act | Is the software statutorily excluded from the device definition? | Excluded if healthy lifestyle intent, unrelated to diagnosis or treatment |
| 3 | FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance | Is the claim substantiated and non misleading? | Every measurement claim needs competent and reliable scientific evidence. This is a validation line item, not a footnote |

## 1. FDA General Wellness Policy

### 1.1 The controlling document

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Title | General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices |
| Issuing body | FDA, Center for Devices and Radiological Health (CDRH) |
| Type | Guidance for Industry and FDA Staff. Nonbinding recommendations |
| Current version date | January 6, 2026 (revised final) |
| Supersedes | The prior final version dated September 27, 2019 |
| Primary locator | fda.gov guidance page "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices"; document download fda.gov/media/90652 |
| Confidence | HIGH. Corroborated across Covington, Faegre Drinker, Arnold & Porter, DLA Piper, King & Spalding, Troutman, Womble, and an American Hospital Association news item dated 2026-01-06 |
| Staleness | Current. Issued inside the 18 month window |

### 1.2 What the policy does

FDA does not intend to examine low risk general wellness products to determine whether they are devices, or, if they are devices, whether they comply with premarket review and other requirements. In practice this is enforcement discretion for products that meet two tests at once: the intended use is a general wellness use, and the product is low risk.

### 1.3 The two categories of permitted general wellness intended use

| Category | Description | Example phrasing that qualifies |
|----------|-------------|--------------------------------|
| Category 1: pure wellness | Claims about maintaining or encouraging a general state of health or a healthy activity, with no reference to disease | Weight management, physical fitness, relaxation, stress management, sleep, mental acuity |
| Category 2: disease referenced healthy lifestyle | Claims that a healthy lifestyle may help reduce the risk of, or help living well with, certain chronic conditions, where that relationship is well understood and accepted | Products that promote physical activity, which as part of a healthy lifestyle may help reduce the risk of certain chronic diseases |

Category 2 is the load bearing one for elder monitoring and pregnancy support, because it permits a product to reference a chronic condition without becoming a device, so long as the reference is framed through the healthy lifestyle relationship and not through diagnosis, treatment, or a diagnostic threshold.

### 1.4 The 2026 revision and why it matters to this program

The January 6, 2026 revision is not cosmetic. The material change, corroborated across multiple independent legal analyses, is that FDA now treats noninvasive products (wearables specifically named) that estimate, infer, or output physiologic parameters (blood pressure, blood glucose, blood oxygen saturation, heart rate variability among them) as eligible for general wellness treatment, provided the product is intended solely for wellness use, poses minimal risk, and avoids disease, diagnostic threshold, or clinical management claims.

Consequence for the program: the estimation and inference of a physiologic quantity is no longer, by itself, the thing that drags a product into device territory. The disease claim or the diagnostic threshold is. This aligns exactly with the input versus inference line in framework section 2. It moves the boundary from "did you compute a physiologic number" to "did you assert a disease from it."

Confidence on the specific parameter list: MEDIUM. The direction and substance are corroborated across at least five independent firm analyses; the exact enumerated parameters and any qualifying conditions require reading the full guidance text, flagged in Open Questions.

### 1.5 Low risk test

A product falls outside the general wellness policy, and is not covered by enforcement discretion, if it presents other than a low risk. The policy excludes products that are invasive, that involve an intervention or technology that may pose a risk to safety if controls are not applied, or that raise novel questions of usability or biocompatibility. A software product that measures, trends, charts, and notifies, and that does not actuate anything on the body, sits comfortably on the low risk side.

## 2. Statutory Software Exclusions: FD&C Act Section 520(o)

### 2.1 The statute

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Authority | Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, section 520(o), codified at 21 U.S.C. 360j(o) |
| Created by | 21st Century Cures Act, section 3060(a), Public Law 114-255 |
| Enacted | December 13, 2016 |
| Effect | Removes five categories of software function from the device definition in FD&C Act section 201(h) |
| Confidence | HIGH for existence, structure, and the 520(o)(1)(B) and 520(o)(1)(E) content. Corroborated by FDA's own software policy materials and multiple firm analyses |
| Staleness | Current but dated. Statute from 2016, in force and unamended on this point. Not stale |

This matters more than the guidance. Guidance is enforcement discretion, which FDA can withdraw. A statutory exclusion removes the product from the device definition as a matter of law. Where a function qualifies under 520(o), it is not a device at all, and the general wellness enforcement discretion analysis becomes a belt and suspenders backstop rather than the primary defense.

### 2.2 The exclusion categories relevant to this program

| Provision | What it excludes | Relevance |
|-----------|------------------|-----------|
| 520(o)(1)(B) | Software intended for maintaining or encouraging a healthy lifestyle and unrelated to the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment of a disease or condition | The primary statutory home for both concepts. The healthy lifestyle wellness carve out |
| 520(o)(1)(C) | Software intended to serve as electronic patient records, where not intended to interpret or analyze records for diagnosis | Covers journaling, symptom logs, self report storage |
| 520(o)(1)(D) | Software intended to transfer, store, convert formats, or display device data and results, unless intended to interpret or analyze that data | Covers surfacing a raw measurement and its trend |
| 520(o)(1)(E) | Clinical decision support software meeting four criteria (see section 3) | Governs any feature that hands a recommendation to a clinician |
| 520(o)(2) | Multi function product rule: a device function does not automatically pull the non device software functions into regulation | Permits a single app to carry both wellness functions and, if ever added, a regulated function, without the wellness functions being swept in |

The 520(o)(1)(B) healthy lifestyle exclusion and the general wellness guidance in section 1 are two expressions of the same policy: the statute states the exclusion, the guidance states how FDA reads "low risk" and "general wellness" in practice.

| Staleness | Current |

### 3.2 The four criteria for non device CDS (520(o)(1)(E))

A software function is excluded from the device definition, and is Non Device CDS, only if it meets all four:

| # | Criterion | Plain reading |
|---|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | Not intended to acquire, process, or analyze a medical image, a signal from an in vitro diagnostic device, or a pattern or signal from a signal acquisition system | If the software reads raw images or raw physiologic signals to reach its output, it fails criterion 1 and is a device |
| 2 | Intended to display, analyze, or print medical information about a patient or other medical information such as peer reviewed studies and clinical practice guidelines | The inputs are medical information, not raw device signal |
| 3 | Intended to support or provide recommendations to a health care professional about prevention, diagnosis, or treatment | The recipient is a clinician, not the patient |
| 4 | Intended to enable the health care professional to independently review the basis for the recommendations, so that the professional does not rely primarily on the software to make a decision | The clinician can see and second guess the reasoning. The software is not a black box verdict |

### 3.3 The trap in criterion 1 and its direct bearing on this program

Criterion 1 is where consumer sensing products most often fail CDS. A product that analyzes a raw accelerometer or PPG signal to produce its output is "processing a pattern or signal from a signal acquisition system," which defeats non device CDS status. This is precisely why the program should not lean on the CDS exclusion for its passive sensing features. The correct home for gait, steadiness, and vitals trending is the general wellness policy plus 520(o)(1)(B), not CDS. CDS becomes relevant only for a future clinician facing feature that operates on already computed medical information and hands a reviewable recommendation to a provider.

### 3.4 The 2026 CDS change

The revision maintains FDA's existing statutory interpretation and does not fundamentally deregulate. It adds an enforcement discretion carve out: a tool that produces a single specific recommendation may fall under enforcement discretion where a health care professional is in the loop, the tool does not analyze the underlying image, it relies only on well understood and accepted sources, the recommendation is clinically appropriate, and all other non device CDS criteria are met. The guidance does not define "clinically appropriate," which leaves the carve out's true scope open. Confidence MEDIUM.

## 4. Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) Framing

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Source | FDA Digital Health Center of Excellence, "Software as a Medical Device (SaMD)"; IMDRF SaMD Working Group framework documents |
| Definition | Software intended to be used for one or more medical purposes that performs those purposes without being part of a hardware medical device. Runs on general purpose computing platforms |
| Risk framework | IMDRF two dimensional framework: significance of the information to the healthcare decision, crossed with the state of the healthcare situation. Yields four categories I through IV, IV highest impact |
| FDA use of it | FDA does not adopt the IMDRF I through IV levels as regulatory classes. It maps intended use and risk onto its own Class I, II, III device classes |
| Confidence | HIGH on the definition and the IMDRF framework structure. MEDIUM on the precise current FDA mapping language |
| Staleness | Framework foundational, current but dated. Not stale |

Why SaMD is in this dossier: it is the regime the program is deliberately staying out of. SaMD is what a wellness product becomes the moment its intended use is a medical purpose, meaning diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment of a disease. The entire positioning strategy is a decision to keep every intended use out of the SaMD definition. Documenting SaMD is documenting the boundary the product is engineered to sit just inside of.

### 5.1 The controlling document

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|

| Staleness | Current but dated. Roughly three and a half years old, in force, foundational. Not stale. Governs the program with full force because health related apps and equipment are named in scope |

### 5.2 The substantiation standard

FTC requires that objective health related claims be supported by competent and reliable scientific evidence, defined as tests, analyses, research, or studies that (1) have been conducted and evaluated in an objective manner by qualified persons and (2) are generally accepted in the profession to yield accurate and reliable results. The evidence must be sufficient in quality and quantity, judged against standards generally accepted in the relevant scientific fields, and considered in light of the entire body of relevant evidence.

| Element | FTC position | Program implication |
|---------|--------------|---------------------|
| Gold standard | Randomized, controlled human clinical testing by relevant experts | For a health outcome claim, a trial is the expectation |
| Quantity | No fixed number of studies. Replication adds weight | One study is thin. Budget for more than one |
| Quality over quantity | Explicitly stated. Quality matters more than count | A single rigorous validation beats several weak ones |
| Animal and in vitro | Cannot, standing alone, substantiate a human health claim | Not a substitute for human validation |
| Consumer surveys | Cannot substantiate an efficacy claim | Testimonials do not count |
| Epidemiological or observational | Accepted only where experts consider it an acceptable substitute and clinical trials are not feasible | Limited fallback |

### 5.3 The distinction that controls the FTC cost

FTC does not care whether the product is a device. It cares whether every stated claim is true and substantiated. The claims this program makes are predominantly measurement claims, not outcome claims, and that distinction sets the substantiation bar.

| Claim type | Example | What FTC requires to substantiate |
|------------|---------|-----------------------------------|
| Measurement claim | "Walking speed is 18 percent below the 30 day baseline" | Evidence the measurement is accurate against a recognized reference standard. An accuracy validation study of the metric, not a clinical outcomes trial |
| Trend claim | "Nighttime bathroom visits rose from 4 to 9 this week" | Evidence the underlying count is accurate. A detection accuracy study |
| Outcome or benefit claim | "Using this product reduces falls" | Competent and reliable scientific evidence of the outcome. A controlled trial. Expensive. Avoid unless funded |
| Disease claim | Any assertion of a named condition | Not an FTC substantiation problem, it is an FDA device problem. Do not make it |

The strategic move is to make measurement and trend claims, which convert the FTC question into a bounded metrology validation cost, and to avoid outcome and disease claims, which would demand a clinical trial. Each retained measurement claim carries a validation study line item into the business case at G3 and G4. This is where the money goes, per framework section 2. The claims matrix (framework deliverable 1) must tag every shipping claim with its required substantiation tier from the table above.

## 6. Consumer Product Precedents to Document

The purpose here is not to prove these features exist. It is to document, with the classification or enforcement basis, exactly why each one ships as a wellness feature and not as a cleared device. Each row establishes a precedent the program's own features sit directly behind.

### 6.1 Apple Watch and iPhone gait, mobility, and walking steadiness

| Field | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| Feature | Mobility Metrics (walking speed, step length, double support time, walking asymmetry) introduced iOS 14 and watchOS 7; Walking Steadiness classification introduced iOS 15 |
| Output | A measured quantity with a trend, plus a Low or Very Low Walking Steadiness classification with a notification |
| How Apple frames it | An added signal for possible fall risk, not a diagnosis. Apple positions the numbers as trend signals and prompts the user to review data and consider talking with a healthcare professional. Health app can suggest exercises |
| Why it ships as wellness | The output is a measured metric and a stability classification with a general notification. It infers no named disease. It hands the pattern to the user and a possible clinician conversation. This is measurement and reference, not diagnosis. Squarely inside FDA general wellness category 1 and 2 and the framework "comfortably inside the lane" list |
| Basis | FDA General Wellness policy plus the input versus inference line. No device clearance is invoked for these metrics |
| Source | Apple Support "Monitor your walking steadiness in Health on iPhone" (support.apple.com/en-us/102504); Apple developer WWDC21 session "Measure health with motion"; Apple Heart and Movement Study basis |
| Confidence | HIGH that it ships this way and is framed this way. MEDIUM on the precise regulatory rationale, which is inferred from Apple's framing and FDA policy rather than a published FDA classification letter for these specific metrics |

Direct read across to the program: gait, stride, sway, sit to stand, and turn count trended against the user's own baseline are the same class of output as Apple's mobility metrics. They ship on the same basis.

### 6.2 Apple Watch and Pixel Watch fall detection with emergency escalation

| Field | Apple Watch | Google Pixel Watch |
|-------|-------------|--------------------|
| Feature | Fall Detection with Emergency SOS | Fall Detection with Emergency Sharing |
| Behavior | On a hard fall with about a minute of immobility, a 30 second countdown then an automatic call to emergency services with location and Medical ID | On a fall with no movement or response for about a minute, an automatic call to emergency services with location |
| How it is framed | A safety feature. Apple states the watch cannot detect all falls and false alarms can occur | A safety feature, part of Personal Safety |
| Why it ships as wellness | The output is an event notification and an escalation to emergency services. "A fall was detected" is a statement that an event occurred, not an inference of a disease. Event notification and escalation to a designated contact or emergency service is expressly inside the framework lane |
| Basis | FDA General Wellness policy. Detection of a physical event plus escalation, no diagnostic claim |
| Source | Apple Support "Use Fall Detection with Apple Watch" (support.apple.com/en-us/108896); Google Pixel Watch Help "Fall Detection"; Google Store safety features |
| Confidence | HIGH on behavior and framing. MEDIUM on regulatory rationale, inferred |

Sharpening counterexample, documented to mark the boundary precisely: Google Pixel Watch Loss of Pulse Detection is not a wellness feature. It went through FDA review (De Novo) before US launch, because detecting cardiac arrest, a specific life threatening physiologic state, and auto escalating is a device function. The contrast is instructive. Fall detection reports a mechanical event and stays wellness. Loss of pulse detection asserts a physiologic emergency state and became a cleared device. The program's fall and event detection features sit on the fall detection side of this line, not the loss of pulse side.

| Feature | Output character | Regulatory status | Lesson |
|---------|------------------|-------------------|--------|
| Fall detection | An event occurred | Wellness, non device | Report the event, escalate, assert no condition |
| Loss of pulse detection | A physiologic emergency state exists | FDA reviewed device | Asserting a specific physiologic condition is a device claim |

## 7. The Input Versus Inference Line, Made Explicit

This is the single controlling test from framework section 2, restated here as the operative rule for counsel and reproduced so the dossier stands alone.

| Principle | Statement | Status |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Self report is not a claim | If the user supplies the data and the app stores, trends, charts, and reflects it back, the app has asserted nothing. Mood journals, symptom logs, sleep diaries, pain scales | Inside the lane |
| Measurement is not a claim about disease | Walking speed, stride length, sway, sit to stand time, turn count, resting heart rate, HRV, temperature are quantities. Trending them, comparing to the user's own baseline, and comparing to a published normative range are measurement and reference | Inside the lane |
| Inference of a named disease from passive data is a claim | The output string is what matters. "This pattern is consistent with depression" is a screening result. Same data, different assertion | Outside the lane. This is the device line |

Worked application, same underlying data, different output string:

| Underlying data | Output that stays inside the lane | Output that crosses the line |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------|------------------------------|
| 14 days of low self reported mood scores | "Your mood scores have been low for 14 straight days, and you flagged this yourself. Consider talking with your clinician." | "This pattern is consistent with depression." |
| Declining walking speed and steadiness | "Walking speed is down 18 percent from your baseline and steadiness is Low." | "You are at high risk of a fall requiring intervention." |
| Rising overnight temperature and resting heart rate | "Signs of strain detected. Your temperature is up 0.5 degrees from baseline." | "You have an infection." |
| A lab value entered by the user | Display the value with the lab's printed reference range and explain what the analyte is | "Your result means you have condition X." |

The design rule that follows: prefer self report for anything affective or cognitive, prefer measurement and trend for anything physical, and let the user, the caregiver, or the clinician draw the conclusion. The product surfaces the pattern and hands it to a human. In this category that posture is both the safest regulatory position and the stronger product.

The narrow set that must be engineered a specific way (validated clinical instruments returned as a scored result, clinical interpretation of a lab value, treatment or medication change instructions, and any output that could cause a user to delay care) is not covered here. It belongs in regulatory_risk_register.md with mitigations, per framework deliverable 3, and is out of scope for this dossier.

## Assumptions Made

1. The general wellness positioning is settled and not to be relitigated. Taken as given from framework section 2. This dossier documents the basis; it does not evaluate the decision.
2. The program's features produce output strings of the measurement, trend, event, or self report class, not disease inference. The dossier's conclusions hold only for features engineered to that standard. Any feature that outputs a named disease inferred from passive data falls outside every precedent here and is a device question, routed to the risk register.
3. Corroboration across multiple independent AmLaw 100 analyses plus the FDA and FTC search indexes is treated as reliable evidence of a primary document's title, date, and substance where the primary text could not be opened directly due to 403 blocking. Flagged wherever it affects a material claim.
4. "Current" is assessed as of the stated date, July 10, 2026. The FDA guidances are six months old and the FTC guidance about three and a half years old. All are treated as in force.
5. The consumer precedent rationales (why each ships as wellness) are inferred from each manufacturer's own framing and disclaimers combined with FDA policy, not from a per feature FDA classification letter, except where a manufacturer states the intended use limitation explicitly (Oura does; Apple and Google frame but do not publish a device rationale).

## Confidence Summary

Overall confidence: HIGH that the positioning is defensible and that the lane is, as of January 6, 2026, materially wider than it was under the 2019 policy. The strongest, most current, and most directly on point authority is the revised General Wellness guidance dated 2026-01-06, which expressly admits noninvasive wearable estimation and inference of physiologic parameters into the wellness lane so long as disease and diagnostic threshold claims are avoided. That single document does more to secure both concepts than any precedent.

Strongest elements (HIGH): the existence, dates, and thrust of the January 6, 2026 FDA General Wellness and CDS revisions; the 520(o) statutory exclusions and their 2016 enactment; the FTC December 2022 guidance, its substantiation standard, and its express coverage of health apps and equipment; the Oura Symptom Radar precedent with its verbatim non device, non diagnostic disclaimer; the input versus inference line.

Weakest elements, requiring the human verification listed in Open Questions before this document is handed to a payer legal team as final: the exact enumerated parameter list in the 2026 General Wellness guidance (MEDIUM), the precise operative text and media identifier of the 2026 CDS guidance (MEDIUM), and the per feature regulatory rationale for the Apple and Google precedents, which is inferred rather than documented by an FDA determination (MEDIUM). None of these weaken the strategic conclusion. They are precision and citation hardening tasks, not open strategic risks.

The one point to underline for counsel: the durable protection is statutory, not discretionary. FD&C Act 520(o)(1)(B) removes qualifying healthy lifestyle software from the device definition as a matter of law. The general wellness guidance is enforcement discretion layered on top. Anchor the position on the statute first and the guidance second.
