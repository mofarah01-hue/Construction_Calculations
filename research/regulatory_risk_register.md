# regulatory_risk_register.md
## Regulatory and Legal Risk Register: Concept A (Elder Home Monitoring) and Concept B (Pregnancy and Parenting Companion)

Governed by `00_framework.md`. This is a risk register, not a list of prohibitions. Every risk carries an engineering or product mitigation, a leading indicator, and a cost line. Both products are positioned as general wellness per framework section 2. That positioning is the primary control against most of what follows. This register documents where the positioning is load bearing and where it can fail.

Prepared 10 July 2026. Confidence tags apply to the risk assessment, not to the underlying legal fact, which is separately cited in the Register Entries sub-table.

---

## Methodology Note: How Likelihood and Impact Were Scored

**Likelihood** is the probability the risk materializes within the first 36 months of commercial operation (G5 to G6), given the mitigations are NOT yet in place. It is scored on the base rate of enforcement or litigation in the category, weighted by how directly a cited precedent maps onto the concept as described.

- HIGH: a directly analogous enforcement action or lawsuit already exists against a comparable product, and the triggering conduct is inherent to the concept as written.
- MEDIUM: precedent exists in an adjacent category, or the trigger is a design choice we can avoid but the naive build would hit.
- LOW: precedent is sparse or the conduct is easily engineered around, but the tail outcome is severe enough to register.

**Impact** is scored on the worst credible outcome: financial exposure, forced product change, and existential threat to the company.

- SEVERE: can end the company (a market withdrawal, a per-plaintiff statutory damage multiplier across a large class, a criminal exposure, or loss of a single dependency the product cannot run without).
- HIGH: material cash penalty, mandatory product redesign, or reputational damage that raises CAC.
- MODERATE: contained cost, a consent decree with audit obligations, or a bounded settlement.

**Scoring inputs are precedent-anchored.** Where a dollar figure or probability could not be verified from a primary source, the cell reads UNKNOWN. Statutory damage ranges (BIPA, CUBI, MHMDA) are cited to statute; realized settlement figures are cited to the settling order. The two are not the same number and are not averaged.

| R1 FDA reclassification (device creep) | A wellness feature drifts into a diagnostic or screening claim, pulling the product inside the section 201(h) device definition and out of the General Wellness enforcement-discretion lane. The FDA General Wellness guidance covers claims about maintaining a healthy lifestyle; the moment an output infers a named, diagnosable condition from passive data, the enforcement discretion does not apply. Precedent that the FDA does reclassify in this exact space: home uterine activity monitors were regulated as Class III, then reclassified Class II Special Controls in 2001, and remain prescription-only with a documented no-benefit evidence base. Device creep is bidirectional and real. | A and B | MEDIUM | SEVERE | Enforce the input-versus-inference line in software, not in prompts. A claims-linter gate in CI that blocks any user-facing string asserting a diagnosable condition from passive data. Self-report stays a journal (stored, trended, shown back). | R2 FTC substantiation (measurement claims) | Any quantitative claim ("walking speed is down 18 percent," "your HRV is below your baseline") is a measurement claim requiring competent and reliable scientific evidence under FTC Health Products Compliance standards. A camera-derived gait metric or a PPG-derived HRV value that is not validated against a recognized reference standard is an unsubstantiated claim. Direct precedent: Aura Labs paid a suspended $595,945 judgment in 2016 for claiming its Instant Blood Pressure app was as accurate as a cuff when it was not. Lumosity paid $2M (from a $50M suspended judgment) in 2016 for cognitive-benefit claims it could not back with human clinical data. | A and B | HIGH | HIGH | Convert every shipped metric into a validation line item. Bench-validate camera gait speed against an instrumented walkway; validate PPG HRV against ECG; validate any BP display against a cuff-only ingestion path (do not compute BP). Where a metric is not yet validated, ship it as a trend of the user's own signal without an accuracy claim, or withhold it. Print reference-range provenance on every normative comparison. | An FTC business-guidance blog post or closing letter naming a wellness measurement app; a competitor settlement on measurement accuracy; internal validation showing error bars wider than the smallest claimed change. | Validation studies are the real spend and land at G3 to G4. Per-metric gait validation against instrumented walkway: UNKNOWN (log in phase1/phase3). This is where the money goes, not a footnote. |
| R3 Product liability: failure to escalate (false negative) | A fall is not detected, or a preeclampsia red flag is not surfaced, and the user is harmed by delayed care. This is the highest-severity failure mode in both products. Note the causation defense that has historically protected PERS vendors: in the Connecticut wrongful-death case the court directed verdict for the PERS company because the plaintiff could not prove the alert would have changed the outcome. That defense is real but it is not a design strategy, and it erodes the moment marketing implies reliability the system does not have. | A (fall, long-lie) and B (preeclampsia, postpartum hemorrhage, self-harm) | MEDIUM | SEVERE | Never position detection as a safety guarantee. Hard-coded red-flag escalation layer that is enforced, logged, and independent of the LLM. Explicit, surfaced disclaimers that the product is not a monitored medical alarm and does not replace emergency services or clinical care. Redundant escalation paths for latency-critical events. Design the marketing claim to match the validated false-negative rate, not the aspiration. Preserve the causation defense by never overstating reliability. | Any near-miss in G3/G4 telemetry where a true event was not escalated; a plaintiff's-bar advertisement targeting a competitor; a state AG inquiry into monitoring reliability claims. | Escalation layer engineering: UNKNOWN weeks. Product liability insurance premium: UNKNOWN, scales with claims made and lands at G5. Legal review of every disclaimer surface: UNKNOWN. |
| R4 State biometric privacy law (BIPA / CUBI / MHMDA) | Concept A derives biometric identifiers (gait signature, face geometry, voiceprint) from continuous in-home video and audio. Illinois BIPA and Texas CUBI require informed written consent before capture of a biometric identifier, and BIPA carries a private right of action with per-violation statutory damages. Realized exposure is not hypothetical: Facebook settled BIPA face-template claims for $650M (2021); Texas settled CUBI face-geometry claims with Meta for $1.4B (2024, largest single-state AG privacy settlement). MHMDA (Washington, in force 31 March 2024) adds a private right of action reaching health-adjacent data with treble damages up to $25,000. | A (primary: biometric capture) and B (health data under MHMDA) | HIGH | SEVERE | Consent architecture as a first-class feature, not a checkbox. Written, informed, revocable consent from the data subject (the resident) captured before any biometric template is generated, with a retention and destruction schedule published in the policy. Prefer on-device (T1) inference so raw biometrics never leave the home; store templates encrypted with a defined deletion trigger. Design explicitly for the resident-does-not-want-to-be-watched case and the cognitive-impairment consent case. Geofence higher-risk features by state where the compliance posture is not yet met. | A BIPA or CUBI class filing against any home-monitoring or wearable vendor; a plaintiff's firm running intake ads in Illinois; MHMDA class filings expanding beyond location-SDK cases; a state adding a private right of action. | Consent architecture and template lifecycle engineering: UNKNOWN weeks (shared with `shared_privacy_security.md`). Per-state legal mapping: UNKNOWN. Statutory exposure if unmitigated is per-subject and uncapped in aggregate; this is the single largest tail liability in the portfolio. |
| R5 Reproductive-health-data legal risk | Concept B holds pregnancy, fertility, mood, and potentially loss/termination data. Post-Dobbs, this data is subpoena and warrant exposed. The Nebraska prosecution (Burgess, 2022) used Facebook messages, obtained by warrant, as primary evidence in an abortion-related case; both defendants pleaded guilty. FTC precedent on selling or leaking this exact data category: Flo Health (2021 order) for sharing fertility data with Facebook and Google; GoodRx (2023) paid a $1.5M civil penalty in the first Health Breach Notification Rule action. MHMDA was written specifically for reproductive data and gives consumers a private right of action. | B (primary) and A (health-adjacent data under MHMDA) | HIGH | SEVERE | Data minimization and short retention as architecture, not policy. Do not retain reproductive-status data longer than the product function requires; offer user-controlled deletion; consider on-device-only storage for the most sensitive fields. Never share health data with advertising or analytics third parties (the Flo and GoodRx trigger). Encrypt such that a warrant to the company yields the least possible plaintext. Publish a law-enforcement-request policy. Treat every partner-facing disclosure (R6) as a reproductive-data disclosure. | Any FTC or state-AG action against a femtech or health app for data sharing; a subpoena or law-enforcement request received; a state expanding reproductive-data liability; a data broker found reselling comparable data. | Data-minimization and retention engineering: UNKNOWN weeks. | R6 Wiretap / two-party consent audio (Concept A) | Concept A's assistant and acoustic sensing record audio in a home where visitors and household members have not consented. California Penal Code 632 and comparable all-party-consent statutes criminalize recording a confidential communication without the consent of all parties; a first violation carries up to a $2,500 fine per violation and up to one year in county jail, plus a civil private right of action. Covert placement (a disguised device) compounds this into a distinct ethical and legal problem. | A | MEDIUM | HIGH | Do not record or persist raw audio by default. Wake-word gated capture, on-device speech-to-text, immediate discard of raw audio, and a visible always-on recording indicator. Onboarding consent that names all-party-consent obligations and instructs the buyer to notify household members and visitors. No covert form factors. Geofence audio features by state consent regime. Treat the microphone as opt-in, not default-on. | A state consumer-protection or wiretap complaint against any home voice device; a visitor complaint in G3/G4 testing; a plaintiff's firm targeting always-listening devices. | Wake-word + on-device STT + discard pipeline: UNKNOWN weeks (overlaps assistant build). Recording-indicator hardware/UX: low, lands at G3. Per-state consent-flow logic: UNKNOWN. |
| R7 Wearable-API dependency (Concept B, vendor revocation) | Concept B's personalization depends on physiological data from a consumer wearable via a third-party API whose terms the vendor controls unilaterally. A vendor can deprecate the API, restrict raw-data access, or forbid the commercial use we intend, and end the product overnight. This is a live, dated event: the legacy Fitbit Web API is being turned down in September 2026, forcing all integrations to migrate to the Google Health API with mandatory user re-consent and no silent migration. Intraday and raw access is already granted only case-by-case with the vendor reserving the right to limit it. | B (primary) and A (if a wearable path is used, per A4) | HIGH | SEVERE | Do not architect a single-vendor dependency. Abstract the wearable behind an internal data-access layer so vendors are swappable. Support at least two independent device ecosystems at launch. Design the product so its core defensible value (self-report plus urgent-warning-sign education per the Concept B insight) functions with zero wearable data, making the wearable an enhancement, not a dependency. Evaluate a white-label or contract-manufactured band as the fallback (see `shared_wearable_data_access.md`). Track vendor terms and deprecation notices continuously. | Any vendor deprecation notice, terms-of-service change restricting commercial use, rate-limit tightening, or acquisition of a wearable vendor by a competitor; the Fitbit September 2026 turndown as a template event. | Abstraction layer plus second-ecosystem integration: UNKNOWN weeks. Fallback band program: separate BOM, UNKNOWN. The cheapest mitigation is the product-design one: make wearable data optional. |
| R8 Content-licensing risk (Concept B) | The grounded knowledge base and the developmental-screening surfaces may incorporate proprietary content. Validated screening instruments and milestone frameworks are frequently copyrighted and trademarked: the Ages and Stages Questionnaire (ASQ-3) is a registered trademark of Paul H. Shipping such content without a license is infringement; embedding a licensed instrument also risks converting a wellness feature into administration of a clinical tool (see R1). | B | MEDIUM | HIGH | Build on free, public, unencumbered sources where they exist (CDC growth charts, CDC Learn the Signs milestone content, ACOG and CDC Hear Her public patient-education materials). | R9 Any-output-that-delays-care (severity, not classification) | Distinct from R3. Even a correctly functioning, non-diagnostic output can cause harm if it reassures a user out of seeking care she needs ("your numbers look normal") or if the LLM answers a 3am danger question wrongly. This is a severity risk that exists regardless of claim classification, and framework section 2 flags it as the failure mode that must be engineered, not disclaimed. | A and B | MEDIUM | SEVERE | A hard-coded red-flag layer that overrides the assistant: enumerated ACOG/CDC urgent maternal warning signs (Concept B) and enumerated emergency cues (Concept A) trigger an immediate direct-to-care response that the LLM cannot suppress or soften. ## Open Questions

1. Exact statutory per-violation damages under Illinois BIPA as currently amended (the 2024 amendment on per-scan accrual changes aggregate exposure materially). Not re-verified to primary this pass. Blocks precise R4 tail sizing.
2. ASQ-3 and other instrument license fees to a figure. Vendor lists pricing but the number was not captured. Blocks R8 cost cell.
3. Whether the assistant's danger-assessment refusal behavior can be enforced deterministically at acceptable latency, or whether residual LLM nondeterminism keeps R9 partially open regardless of engineering. Architecture question for Phase 3B / Phase 4A.
4. State-by-state map of all-party-consent audio regimes and biometric statutes for the geofencing mitigations in R4 and R6. Needed before G5 multi-state launch.
5. Product-liability insurance availability and premium for a wellness-positioned monitoring product making escalation-adjacent claims. Blocks R3 cost cell and the G5 model.
6. Whether MHMDA and successor state laws treat gait or activity data as "consumer health data." If yes, R4 and R5 expand to Concept A more broadly than currently scored.

## Assumptions Made

1. Assumed the general-wellness positioning holds as a settled strategic decision (framework section 2) and scored R1 as MEDIUM rather than HIGH on that basis. If the positioning is relitigated or a feature ships outside the lane, R1 likelihood rises to HIGH. Impact if wrong: the central control against R1 through R3 weakens simultaneously.
2. Assumed both concepts will ship at least one quantitative measurement claim (gait speed for A, HRV or normative physiological curves for B). This is what makes R2 HIGH. If a concept ships pure self-report and event-detection only, R2 drops to MEDIUM for that concept.
3. Assumed Concept A uses audio (assistant plus acoustic sensing). If audio is cut entirely, R6 falls away. This is a live architecture fork in Concept A Phase 2.
4. Assumed Concept B depends on a consumer wearable in the naive build. The framework and brief B4 already argue the product should not require it; R7 is scored on the dependency existing, and the primary mitigation is removing the dependency.
5. Assumed realized settlement figures (Facebook $650M, Meta $1.4B) are indicative of tail magnitude, not of expected value. Expected value is far lower and was not computed; likelihood and impact are scored separately and not multiplied into a dollar figure here.
6. Treated CDC and ACOG public patient-education content as free and unencumbered for the R8 mitigation. Verified in principle from framework; specific reuse terms not re-fetched this pass.

## Confidence Summary

Overall confidence in this register: HIGH on the existence, direction, and precedent-anchoring of every risk; MEDIUM on the cost cells, which are dominated by UNKNOWNs that belong to the development-plan and business-case phases and were correctly left unresolved rather than invented.

Strongest entries: R4 (biometric privacy) and R5 (reproductive data), both anchored to primary agency orders, statute, and a criminal case with dated, verified settlement and penalty figures. R2 and R7 are also primary-anchored (FTC orders; vendor deprecation notice with a hard date).

Weakest entries: R3 and R9 on the quantitative side. Product-liability exposure and delayed-care severity are real and severe but resist dollar estimation without insurance quotes and a characterized false-negative rate, neither of which exists before G3/G4. The causation defense in S6 is genuine but fragile and should not be leaned on. R1 confidence is MEDIUM because it is contingent on the wellness positioning holding, which is an inherited strategic assumption, not a finding this register validated.

Every material legal fact is cited to a source with a URL and a date. No cost or likelihood figure was invented; unverifiable ones read UNKNOWN and are listed in Open Questions.
